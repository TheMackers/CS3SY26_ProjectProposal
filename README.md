### Project Description:

A game where you play as a powerful leader trying to save a struggling nation, but the game's narration tricks you into justifying war crimes, human rights abuses, and other atrocities that a dictator, generally does. This game would show how dictatorships can be justified through nationalism, false narratives, restoring law and order, national growth, and etc. Its main parent class, Citizen, has attributes name, happiness level (1-5), health (1-3), and has four sub-classes: Official, Workers, Students, and Rebels.

- Officials speak to the user (Who can then present the user with their false narrative, fake data, or false justifications and the options attached to them, and order workers and students in accordance with use inputs.
- Workers can strike and join rebellions, depending on their awareness level, happiness level, and health levels, which can affect the user's legitimacy as a leader.
- Students can also do the same things as Workers, which is strike or join the Rebels, and they can also educate workers to increase their awareness, potentially destabilizing the user's legitimacy as a leader based on their actions.
- Rebels can attack you based on membership levels, decreasing the happiness level of your citizens, and can defend both their physical positions and ideological doctrines so yours weaken too, all affecting the user's legitimacy as a leader.

This game aims to be accessible to around middle school and higher in age range all the way to the "old" (for lack of a better term) age ranges, for anyone who can read and understand concepts at the ideological level, which usually starts at around Grade 5 - 6 with effective schooling, can be educated of the concepts highlighted above and learn about how to spot dictatorial acts and dictatorships itself.

### Goals:

The aim of this project is to show through this game how misguided information and misleading advice can manipulate decisions, how neglecting effects can change the outlook on policies and decisions, and how narratives could be used to "justify" things such as human rights violations. We want the players to get a better understanding on the dynamics between leaders and groups of people, and to make better decisions as leaders or judge more flaws in current leadership.

- Objective 1: Accurately show the effects of the leader's decisions and how people react to them.
- Objective 2: Show the contrast between how policies can be presented and the actual effects and long-term consequences that come from them.
- Objective 3: Discuss how the above classes would accurately interact with each other (how much of happiness level each class' actions give or take from other classes, etc.), with respect to real world relationships, historical examples, and the nature of the simplification that the game had to do.
